## 1. Executive summary

**Can this reach $50/day ($1,500/mo)? Yes — but not soon, and not on ads alone.** The realistic path is affiliate/referral revenue first, display ads second, sponsorship third.

| Horizon | Realistic monthly revenue | What drives it |
|---|---|---|
| Month 0–3 | **$0–50** | Indexing, 0–2k sessions/mo. Affiliate approvals trickle in; near-zero conversions. |
| Month 4–6 | **$100–400** | Long-tail rankings start; 5k–20k pageviews/mo; first payouts clear hold periods. |
| Month 7–12 | **$300–1,200** | 20k–60k pageviews/mo; AdSense live; affiliate conversions compounding. |
| Month 13–24 | **$1,000–2,500+ (possible)** | 60k–150k pageviews/mo plus a working affiliate funnel. |

Three hard dependencies: (1) the site actually ranks — a brand-new domain typically needs **4–8 months for meaningful organic traffic**, and most new pages never reach the top 10 in their first year ([searchpod.com](https://searchpod.com/answers/how-long-does-it-take-a-new-website-to-rank-on-google), [directree.io](https://www.directree.io/blog/how-to-rank-new-website-google-timeline)); (2) affiliate programs approve a new, low-traffic site; (3) the daily price pipeline stays reliable so pages are genuinely useful rather than thin.

**Do not treat $50/day as a promise.** It is a 12–24 month outcome requiring consistent publishing and link-building. Expect the first $100 in month 4–6, not month 1.

## 2. Revenue model comparison with real math

Assumptions: 1 session ≈ 1.3 pageviews; 60–70% Tier-1 traffic (US/UK/CA/AU — geo drives RPM heavily); outbound click-through from comparison pages 3–8%; outbound click → paid signup 0.5–2%.

### 2a. Display ads — a pure volume game

AdSense RPM for tech/developer content is commonly benchmarked at roughly **$5–$25 page RPM**, with technology/software cited at **$10–$25** ([calculations.tools](https://calculations.tools/finance/google-adsense-calculator)) and tech blogs at **$5–$20** ([techconda.com](https://www.techconda.com/2026/02/adsense-rpm-benchmarks.html)); premium managed networks reportedly reach **$18–$35 RPM** on the same traffic ([organicarbitrage.com](https://organicarbitrage.com/articles/adsense-earnings-per-niche)). Plan on **$8 RPM** for a new site.

| Page RPM | Pageviews/mo for $1,500 | Pageviews/day |
|---|---|---|
| $5 | 300,000 | ~10,000 |
| $8 | 187,500 | ~6,250 |
| $12 | 125,000 | ~4,110 |
| $18 | 83,300 | ~2,780 |
| $25 | 60,000 | ~2,000 |
| $35 | 42,900 | ~1,430 |

**Read this as: ads cannot be the early answer.** 6,250 pageviews/day is a mid-size publisher — reachable in 12–24 months, not in 90 days.

### 2b. Affiliate / referral — works at low traffic, the real early engine

**Funnel arithmetic to hold yourself to:** 100,000 pageviews × 5% outbound CTR = 5,000 provider clicks × 1% conversion = **50 signups/mo**. At a $30 blended commission that is **$1,500/mo from affiliate alone** — but it requires the pageviews *and* the approvals.

### 2c. Sponsored placements and later-stage revenue

- **Sponsored slot:** a fixed monthly fee for a clearly labeled, disclosed placement. Tech-audience CPM benchmarks run **$50–$180** ([adcommons.com](https://adcommons.com/articles/newsletter-ad-costs-2025)); a site at 30k pageviews/mo can plausibly ask **$300–$800/mo per sponsor** (an assumption, validated only by what a sponsor signs).
- **Newsletter sponsorship:** mid-size B2B primary placements are reported at **$1,100–$5,000 per send** ([mediaintercept.com](https://www.mediaintercept.com/post/newsletter-sponsorships-formats-pricing-and-tracking)) — realistic only above ~5,000 engaged subscribers.
- **Paid data/API tier (year 2):** sell the normalized multi-provider price dataset (CSV/JSON snapshot or metered API) at $29–$99/mo; 20–50 subscribers = $600–$5,000/mo. Highest margin, and the option that turns a content site into a business. Do not attempt before the pipeline is stable.

**Mix that actually reaches $1,500/mo:** ~50% affiliate, ~30% ads, ~20% sponsorship/paid data. Stack channels; don't bet on one.

## 6. Compliance

- **FTC affiliate disclosure.** A material connection must be disclosed **clearly and conspicuously** — "difficult to miss and easily understandable by ordinary consumers" (16 CFR 255.0(f), [ecfr.gov](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255)). Suggested wording, placed **above the first affiliate link**, visible without scrolling:
  > *"Some links on this page are affiliate links. If you sign up through them, we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. This never affects which providers we show or how we rank them."*
  The same disclosure must follow the links into the newsletter and social posts.
- **AdSense / Google Publisher Policies.** No ads on pages that are mostly copied or auto-generated without added value ([Google Publisher Policies](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/10502938)); no invalid clicks or "click here"-style encouragement. Programmatic pages are safe only when each adds original data, context or comparison — a thin matrix of near-duplicate pages risks the **scaled content abuse** policy ([Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)). Note that `site.json` ships with `ads.enabled: false` — keep ads off until each template passes this test.
- **ads.txt** is recommended rather than mandatory, but should exist with the correctly formatted publisher line ([AdSense ads.txt guide](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/12171612)).
- **Newsletter / privacy.** Need explicit consent language, a working unsubscribe, and a privacy policy that names the email processor ([privacyterms.io](https://privacyterms.io/privacy-policy-for-email-newsletter)). GDPR requires a lawful basis and consent for marketing email to EU/UK subscribers; CCPA/CPRA grants California users the right to opt out of the sale or sharing of personal data ([oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa)). Add a consent/CMP banner if EU ad traffic becomes material.
- **Ranking integrity rule (non-negotiable):** paid or sponsored placements must be labeled, must never change the ordering of price tables or "cheapest" results, and must never be presented as editorial. Sponsors buy a labeled slot, never a rank.

## 7. Measurement — weekly scorecard

| Metric | Why it matters | Healthy direction |
|---|---|---|
| Sessions + pageviews | Baseline for ad math | +15–30% MoM early |
| Indexed pages (GSC) | Is the programmatic SEO working at all | ≥60% of submitted URLs indexed |
| Impressions + average position (GSC) | Leading indicator before clicks | Impressions rising even at poor positions |
| Outbound provider clicks | Your real monetization currency | Rising, tracked as an analytics event |
| Outbound CTR per 1,000 sessions | Measures page usefulness | >30 clicks/1k sessions |
| Affiliate signups / conversions | The revenue funnel | 0.5–1% of outbound clicks |
| Revenue per 1,000 sessions (RPS) | The single most important number | Target $15–$40+ combined RPS |
| Newsletter subscribers | Owned audience; enables sponsorship | Rising weekly |

**Decision rule (day 90, then monthly):**
- **Double down** — indexed ≥50% of submitted, impressions rising MoM, at least one affiliate program converting → publish 3× the page types that earn impressions; apply to the next ad tier.
- **Iterate** — traffic grows but outbound clicks or conversions are flat → pages inform but don't persuade. Fix CTAs, add comparison tables and calculators.
- **Pivot** — at day 180, indexed <20% of submitted and sessions <500/mo → the keyword set or page depth is wrong. Rebuild around fewer, deeper high-intent comparison pages, or pivot toward the paid-data/API business where traffic volume matters less.

## 8. Honest risks

1. **SEO timeline (highest risk).** New domains need 4–8 months for meaningful organic traffic, and most new pages never reach the top 10 in year one. There is no reliable way to compress this — only consistent publishing, fresh data, and genuine links.
2. **Third-party API dependency — ec2.shop.** The pipeline depends on a free public API ([ec2.shop](https://ec2.shop/)) with no SLA and no signup; it is also the sole entry in `site.json → data_sources`. If it changes, rate-limits or disappears, the daily update — and the site's core value proposition — breaks. Mitigation: cache the last-good snapshot, keep the build deployable from cache, and hold AWS's own free [Price List Bulk API](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/awsaccountbilling/latest/aboutv2/price-changes.html) as a fallback.
3. **AWS ToS / trademark considerations.** AWS's site terms restrict use of its marks in ways that could cause confusion or disparage AWS, and grant only a limited license to access the AWS site ([AWS Site Terms](https://aws.amazon.com/terms/)); the Price List API is offered under beta/participation terms. Guardrails: use AWS names only in plain descriptive text, no AWS logos, no implication of partnership or endorsement, cite source and date on every price page, and never present figures as authoritative. If AWS objects, the alternatives catalog remains your own content.
4. **Affiliate rejections and payout lag.** New sites with no traffic get rejected or approved at the lowest tier, and many programs pay only after a 30-day refund window, two paid invoices, or a 90-day activity check — so **revenue lags signups by 1–3 months**. Apply broadly, let an empty `ref_url` render nothing, and never let revenue influence rankings.
5. **Moving ad-network thresholds.** Ezoic went from 10k to 250k monthly users; Raptive dropped to 25k. Any plan built on one network's threshold is fragile — plan around AdSense plus Monumetric/Raptive, and re-check requirements before applying.
